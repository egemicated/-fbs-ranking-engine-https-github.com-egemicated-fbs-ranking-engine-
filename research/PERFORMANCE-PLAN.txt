FBS INTELLIGENCE: A SEPARATE PERFORMANCE VIEW

Recommendation
Build the statistics component as a third view with a different question:
  Strength of Record: What record did the team earn against its schedule?
  Performance: How efficiently did the team play in the games we measured?
  Predictive strength: What should we expect in the next matchup?

Keep the approved SOR calculation and existing forecast model intact. Do not
blend the three scores into a hidden consensus. A team can earn a strong record
while playing less efficiently, or play well in a loss. That disagreement is
useful information rather than a reason to force the ranks to agree.

First deliverable
Inventory the actual free data before choosing a formula. Count measured games
against every eligible game in the SOR snapshot, not just against the games for
which statistics happened to download. Check both teams, FCS/opponent context,
identities, retrieval dates, available fields and opportunity denominators.
Missing is not zero. A missing box score is not an average performance.

Start with the simplest defensible measurement
If reproducible, well-covered play data is available, examine offensive EPA per
scrimmage play and defensive EPA allowed. EPA measures how a play changes its
team's expected scoring position, rather than treating every yard identically.
Use a pinned model artifact; different EPA model generations give different
answers. Audit its inputs and training dates. We must not import polls, recruiting
or betting information through an apparently innocent advanced-stat column.

If only basic game boxes are available, start with offensive yards per play and
yards per play allowed. Call it a scrimmage-efficiency prototype. It does not
fully measure short fields, turnovers, situational execution or special teams.
It is a useful first component, not automatically a complete third ranking.

Opponent adjustment
The initial executable candidate fits one opponent-adjusted net-YPP graph.
It does not yet estimate separate offensive and defensive unit grades. A future
unit model should fit offensive units against the defensive units actually faced,
with shrinkage and a declared reference, and show those components separately.
More plays must not earn points simply because a team plays faster, but more
observed plays can affect precision. Compare game- and play-weighted approaches
in a written older-season experiment instead of assigning attractive weights.

Test venue and subdivision effects in the new metric's own units. The existing
SOR FBS/FCS coefficient measures win log-odds; it is not a yards/play or EPA/play
adjustment. Retain opponents individually, and inspect residual group bias and
schedule connections. Do not turn unknown opponents into FCS teams or subtract
an arbitrary fixed percentage from every FCS game.

Account for the game correctly
Sacks already affect college rushing/total-yard accounting. Reconcile the source
before reconstructing totals, and do not subtract sack yardage a second time.
An EPA measure already contains a turnover's observed effect. Do not add another
turnover bonus, or erase a real turnover under the label of luck. A separately
named luck-adjusted forecast experiment is a different question.

Start with an explicit all-eligible-plays policy. Kneels, spikes, penalties and
no-plays need documented treatment. A garbage-time sensitivity must use pre-play
state or an audited frozen model, not the final winning margin or Vegas-based WP.
Without period/play data, do not pretend full-game box totals exclude overtime.

Keep special teams visible as a coverage/component issue. Raw field-goal or punt
percentages alone do not establish a fair unit grade. Any eventual total measure
needs consistent attribution, opportunity counts and common-unit accounting so
that a defensive or return touchdown is not counted twice.

Validation and release
After the inventory, write the exact experiment protocol before reading its
rankings. Select general settings on covered older seasons, using chronological
splits and cutoff-safe upstream models. Historical data already inspected in
this project is retrospective evidence, even when a new statistic is added.

Compare opponent-adjusted performance with a simple unadjusted-rate baseline.
Check future efficiency error, subdivision bias, missingness, sparse samples and
meaningful game-level disagreements. Tests must cover identity/orientation,
denominators, no double counting, deterministic reproduction and future leakage.
Plays are related within games and teams repeat across weeks; do not publish
independent-play error bars as if they were calibrated uncertainty intervals.

Freeze a candidate before collecting genuinely future evidence. Neither matching
the polls nor producing familiar Top25 teams selects a formula. Better next-game
efficiency prediction also does not prove a uniquely correct performance order.

Cost and operation
Stay free. Prefer verified cached/bulk data, or a supported bulk-season endpoint,
over a request for every team/game. Current weekly operation uses two successful
CFBD calls; no new calls are authorized or performed by this document. Any later
collector must record its call budget and remain within the user's free allowance.
Use the existing Sunday 8 AM Mountain refresh. Public clicks keep reading stored
snapshots. Save measured data, coverage and model versions separately from SOR.

Current executable prototype
research/performance_model.py accepts paired offensive yards/play observations
and explicit ridge/homeYpp settings. Each matched game contributes one net-YPP
rate difference. A ridge graph estimates opponent-adjusted team differences in
the same YPP units. Raw offense/defense rates are diagnostics, not separate
adjusted unit grades. Idle teams stay unranked; sparse samples and minimum play
counts are reported without discarding short observed games.

The declared settings-selection experiment uses 2014-2017 for the global venue
response, selects ridge from [0.5, 1, 2, 4, 8] on chronological 2018-2019 efficiency
error, and evaluates 2023-2025 later. The home's affine effect can be reconstructed
exactly from otherwise identical fits at homeYpp 0 and 1. No 2026 performance,
poll or win/loss outcome selects those settings. This is not a complete unit or
all-stats grade. Source reconstruction and coverage must still pass their audits.

The offline experiment selected ridge 2 and homeYpp 0.2925614235611466 from the
declared older years. These settings are research results, not an approved public
ranking. See results/performance/REPORT.txt for coverage, validation and remaining
subdivision bias. No paid service or production ranking change is enabled.
