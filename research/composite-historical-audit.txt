Design findings
1. Fixed equal thirds and fixed pairwise equal-half ablations make this an
   interpretable comparison. They are design choices, not evidence of optimal
   weights. No poll target or extra SOS term is needed.
2. Tie-aware percentiles solve incompatible score units. They discard score
   magnitude and give correlated evidence repeated influence; equal weights do
   not mean three independent votes. The 0-100 index is not a probability.
3. All components are normalized on the same eligible FBS population at each
   preweek checkpoint. Missing or idle Performance teams are not zero-filled.
   Primary targets require four prior outcome and measured Performance games
   for both teams; report early exclusions and native coverage separately.
4. Completed FBS-FBS outcome games define targets even when the target game's
   Performance statistics are unavailable. This avoids a future-stat-coverage
   filter that could improve apparent accuracy selectively.
5. Replay graph, cutoff, game identities and venue are reconciled across
   components. Only fully earlier processed parent weeks with a 24-hour
   kickoff buffer enter a checkpoint. Later-corrected records and full-season
   rosters still make this reconstructed retrospective availability.
6. Predictive Strength intentionally retains an earlier own-team prior.
   Performance retains only venue/subdivision estimates from the immediately
   previous completed season and resets individual team effects. SOR retains
   no earlier own-team achievement. Reusing a 2025 prior in a 2024 replay or
   using final target-season scores would be leakage; the replay rejects this.
7. Raw ordering accuracy gives ties half credit and asks a neutral-strength
   question. Venue-aware Brier/log loss use the same older-only logistic
   architecture for every model. Its parameters are trained separately for
   each index on 2018-2019 only. Existing uncalibrated Strength probabilities
   must not replace that matched comparison.
8. The Top25 subgroup is the common union of preweek component Top25 sets,
   with boundary ties included. It is not defined from end-season rankings or
   separately selected per model.
9. Pairing by game and resampling complete season/type/week blocks preserve
   direct model comparisons. Shared teams connect different weeks; two main
   seasons are a small evidence base. The reported intervals are sensitivity
   summaries, not independent prospective significance claims.
10. Report pooled and individual years against every standalone, especially
    the strongest one. Pairwise ablations diagnose redundancy; they are not
    permission to select new winning weights after inspecting these years.

Implementation checks observed
- Outcome/Performance target IDs and parent eligible graphs must agree.
- Any target ID in a training graph is rejected.
- Duplicate targets, mismatched seasons, future priors and impossible source
  timing are rejected.
- Performance's direct normal-equation fit is checked against the deployed
  Schur implementation on representative checkpoints.
- Outcome replay has a Node parity test covering SOR, Strength, FCS, neutral
  venue, overtime and previous-team history.
- Percentile tie groups use a fixed score anchor, not transitive chaining.
- Logistic fitting checks design rank, finite coefficients, final gradient
  and Hessian condition diagnostics. No evaluation-year refit is permitted.
- Production protection is hash based; source, prior and code provenance are
  fingerprinted for review.

Interpretation
Future winner accuracy can measure forecast usefulness, but cannot establish
the uniquely correct earned-achievement order. The triple blend is a hybrid:
it intentionally adds prior own-team strength and margin evidence to SOR's
current-season achievement. Human-poll-free does not mean assumption-free.
The tested seasons and current Performance variant were already inspected;
these results cannot be described as untouched validation. A separately
frozen trial with scores saved before kickoff is needed for new prospective
evidence. No automatic live replacement follows from this experiment.

Coverage and conclusion
The 2024-2025 parent universe contains 1,606 FBS-FBS games. Of those, 87
have not yet reached common support and 364 additional games are early-only,
leaving 1,155 primary games. Outcome-only four-game eligibility would contain
1,158, so measured Performance availability excludes three additional 2024
games from primary comparison. These counts must accompany the skill results.

The triple improves on SOR and Performance as a forecasting index in this
main retrospective sample, but shows no clear forecasting advantage over
Predictive Strength. Its slight pooled proper-score gains are uncertain and
do not persist in supplementary 2023. Keep the triple experimental; retain
SOR's separate achievement role and Strength's forecasting role. Do not
replace the live formulas or choose new weights based on these same results.
