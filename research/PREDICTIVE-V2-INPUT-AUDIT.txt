PREDICTIVE V2: ORIGINAL INPUT AUDIT

Decision
Build the first research challenger from verified recorded counts and our own
feature arithmetic and fitted opponent/prediction model. Standard measures such
as yards per play need reproducible implementation, not reinvention of their
names. A provider metric is acceptable only when its formula, inputs, population,
timing and numerical reconstruction meet our declared contract. A similar Top25
is not evidence of matching methods.

What the existing Performance model actually uses
Our code consumes provider offensive/defensive success fractions and play
counts, checks reciprocal pairs and reconstructs integer successes. It does not
use numeric PPA values, betting lines, polls or provider team ratings. Our code
does the individual-opponent and venue/subdivision adjustment.

There is nevertheless an indirect provider dependency: the inspected advanced
game query keeps only plays with a non-null PPA and then aggregates stored
success flags. Thus current Performance is a provider-aggregate benchmark, not
our independently reconstructed success rate on every raw scrimmage play.
Turning off garbage-time filtering does not remove that eligibility condition.
Preserve this frozen benchmark and its limitations; exclude that aggregate from
the new original-count candidate until a separate raw-play rebuild is audited.

Provider model boundaries
CFBD documents PPA, WEPA, CORE, Elo, SRS and win probabilities as model outputs;
its exact production artifacts are not fully public. CFBD's explosiveness also
uses PPA. The new candidate should not import these, CPOE/completion models,
polls, subjective recruiting/portal ratings or spread-derived probabilities.
Our own original raw-gain explosive rate would be a different named measure.

Reliable first inputs
The existing cached boxes contain recorded passing/rushing yards and attempts,
completions, first downs, conversion made/opportunity pairs, turnovers, penalties
and possession time. These are SportsDataverse's ESPN raw box-score release,
not CFBD advanced outputs; preserve that provenance and exact manifest hashes.
Compute our rates from counts. Require paired IDs and mirrored opponents, keep
unknown/missing fields unavailable, and check component totals. College sacks
already affect the source rushing totals; do not subtract them twice or invent
dropbacks without sack counts. Team-wide penalty yards per offensive play are
an exposure proxy, not measured offensive-only penalty cost.

What these boxes cannot supply
They do not reconstruct individual-play success, explosive frequency, drive
starting position, scoring opportunities, regulation-only statistics or complete
special-teams value. Those require additional verified recorded opportunities.
Possession seconds divided by plays is not automatically an exact tempo measure.

Player information
Raw player production can be useful, but attribution and timing are essential.
Passing and receiving credit overlap, and team-only/unattributed plays mean
player totals need not sum to a team total. Final-season player numbers, updated
rosters and later transfer facts cannot predict an earlier game. The returning
production endpoint includes PPA fields; rebuild any original version from dated
raw production and roster identity facts. Do not assume complete historic
injury, lineup or snap-count coverage.

Play-level rebuild, when data supports it
Choose the play population before evaluation, including sacks, no-plays,
penalties, kneels, spikes and overtime. Recompute successful plays from recorded
down/distance/gain and explicitly handled offensive scores. A scoring flag alone
can describe a defensive touchdown and cannot make every scoring play an
offensive success. Never filter our raw sample by provider PPA availability or
provider model-derived garbage-time/win-probability fields.
